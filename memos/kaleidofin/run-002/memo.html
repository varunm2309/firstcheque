Kaleidofin

https://kaleidofin.com

A Chennai-based fintech that pairs an AI-driven credit-scoring platform (ki score) with a licensed NBFC to extend formal credit to underbanked women and small businesses in India.

Track

The founding team has strong domain pedigree and the NBFC subsidiary reached standalone break-even in February 2025, but the consolidated group is still loss-making and asset quality has weakened sharply in the last reported year. Worth revisiting once the next audited year shows whether the delinquency rise was temporary and whether consolidated losses are narrowing.

Business model

Kaleidofin Private Limited (KPL) owns a data-driven credit-scoring model, ki score, built on a claimed database of 35 million customers and used to underwrite loans.1 It monetises this in two ways: a Lending-as-a-Service layer that sells credit assessment (ki score, ki view) and customer-engagement tools to banks, MFIs and NBFCs, and a wholly owned NBFC subsidiary, Kaleidofin Capital Private Limited (KCPL), that lends directly using a branchless, partner-originated model through around 15 originator partners.12 KCPL's loan book covers agricultural, women-entrepreneur, micro-entrepreneur and small-business loans.1 The company reports serving over 3 million users across 17 states through 50 channel partners, of whom 97% are women and about 1 million run small businesses.4

Why now

KCPL's NBFC arm reached standalone break-even in February 2025 after several years of scaling losses, and its assets under management grew from INR 45 Cr (March 2023) to INR 114 Cr (March 2024) to INR 140 Cr (December 2024).1 The group raised roughly INR 156 Cr of fresh equity in FY25 across multiple tranches, lifting tangible net worth to about INR 190 Cr, and in June 2026 KCPL took on its first international impact-debt facility, a USD 5 million four-year Orange Bond-certified debenture from an IIX-backed vehicle, targeting clean-energy and financial-inclusion lending.13 In May 2026 the company also closed a KES 276 million agri-finance securitisation in Kenya, its first disclosed move outside India.7 Together these suggest a company moving from a single-market NBFC into a multi-market, diversified-funding credit-infrastructure business, though it is unclear from public sources how much management attention and capital this international push is drawing away from the core India franchise.

Market and competition

Kaleidofin operates in India's underbanked/informal-credit segment, where its stated competition is less about other well-funded fintechs and more about informal-sector alternatives such as chit funds, jewellers and daily deposit schemes; named platform competitors mentioned by one aggregator are FinBox, TransBnk and Linedata.6 That aggregator data was retrieved via a search-engine summary rather than a direct page fetch and should be treated as a lower-confidence, unverified secondary source pending direct confirmation.

Traction and team

On a CARE Ratings consolidated basis, KPL's total income was INR 12 Cr in FY23, INR 29 Cr in FY24, and INR 32 Cr for the first nine months of FY25 (unaudited), against consolidated losses of INR 23 Cr, INR 32 Cr and INR 19 Cr respectively over the same periods.1 On a standalone basis, the NBFC subsidiary KCPL was profitable in FY23 and FY24 (small PAT in both years) and reached break-even in February 2025.1 Asset quality has weakened: 90+ day delinquency rose from 0.02% as of March 2024 to 1.89% as of December 2024, which CARE Ratings attributes partly to broader stress in the microfinance sector rather than to Kaleidofin specifically.1 The leadership team has unusually deep domain pedigree for this segment: CEO Sucharita Mukherjee previously ran credit-derivatives structuring at Morgan Stanley and Deutsche Bank and was founding CEO of Northern Arc Capital, while KCPL CEO Puneet Gupta co-founded the IFMR group (now Northern Arc Capital / Dvara Group) and was on ICICI Bank's founding microfinance team.1

Three risks and possible mitigants

  1. The consolidated group is still loss-making (INR 32 Cr loss in FY24, INR 19 Cr loss in 9MFY25) even though the NBFC subsidiary reached standalone break-even.
    Possible mitigant / question to press on: Ask management for a consolidated (not just KCPL-standalone) path-to-breakeven timeline and what specifically at the KPL parent level (the ki score licensing business, corporate overhead) is driving the gap.
  2. 90+ day delinquency jumped from 0.02% to 1.89% in nine months, which CARE Ratings partly attributes to sector-wide microfinance stress rather than company-specific issues, but the distinction matters a great deal for underwriting quality.
    Possible mitigant / question to press on: Ask for the most recent delinquency data (post-December 2024), a breakdown by originator partner and geography, and how much of the increase is attributable to ki score-accepted vs ki score-rejected-but-disbursed loans.
  3. The lending business depends on roughly 15 third-party originator partners under a branchless model with first-loss default guarantees (FLDG); concentration in any one partner, or a partner's own financial stress, could disrupt the loan book.
    Possible mitigant / question to press on: Ask for partner-level concentration (percentage of AUM from the top 3-5 partners) and FLDG coverage terms.
  4. No public source disclosed a company valuation, monthly revenue, or a cap table, so standard venture-style metrics (revenue multiple, ARPU, dilution) cannot be computed from public information alone.
    Possible mitigant / question to press on: Request the current cap table, most recent monthly MIS, and the valuation basis of the FY25 equity tranches directly from the company.

What would change this recommendation

A move toward Meet would need at least one more audited period showing consolidated (not just standalone-subsidiary) profitability alongside continued AUM growth, plus evidence that the delinquency spike identified in the CARE Ratings report has stabilised or reversed. A move toward Pass would follow from a further deterioration in asset quality, a stalled or down-round equity raise, or evidence that the international expansion is diverting capital from a struggling core business.

Five questions for the founders

  1. What is the plan and timeline to reach consolidated (KPL-level, not just KCPL-standalone) profitability?
  2. What specifically drove 90+ day delinquency from 0.02% in March 2024 to 1.89% in December 2024, and has it stabilised since?
  3. What is the current post-money valuation and cap table following the roughly INR 156 crore raised across FY25?
  4. What share of AUM comes from your largest originator partners, and what happens to the loan book if one of them exits?
  5. How does the Kenya agri-securitisation and the new IIX debt facility fit into capital allocation priorities relative to the core India underbanked-lending business?

This is a plausible but not confident fit: the RDI Scheme is aimed at private-sector R&D in sunrise/strategic domains, and Kaleidofin's activity here looks more like applied fintech infrastructure deployment than frontier R&D, so eligibility should not be assumed from this theme match alone.
